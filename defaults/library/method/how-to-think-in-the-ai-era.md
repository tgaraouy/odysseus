# How to Think in the AI Era — the method behind the Discovery panel

> Source: *How to Think in the AI Era* (crash course). This is the methodology the
> Discovery thinking-panel agents are built on. Held inside Odysseus so the agents —
> and you — work from the same playbook.

## The one rule
**The deliverable is never the answer. It's the documented evidence of thinking.**
AI produces a polished answer in seconds; what makes the work trustworthy is the written
record of *how you judged it*. Person A forms her own view first, pushes back, notes what
would change her mind — she can defend every decision. Person B lets AI's first answer
become her opinion — and can't.

## The six disciplines (each kills one AI failure mode)
1. **Prediction Lock** — *Predict before you prompt.* Before opening AI, write four lines:
   (1) what the decision is really about, (2) the one fact that would help most, (3) your
   position now + reason, (4) confidence % + the exact answer that would flip you. Keep 3-4
   private from the AI (if it knows your commitment, it just agrees). **Kills:** AI's first
   answer silently becoming your opinion.
2. **Reasoning Receipt** — *Document every decision.* For each claim that enters your work,
   one row: label **ACCEPT / REJECT / MODIFY / SURFACED / MISSED** + why. A receipt that's
   all ACCEPT isn't thinking. **Kills:** not being able to defend the work later.
3. **Error Taxonomy** — *Predict where errors hide.* Hunt six mistakes by name, not "is this
   good?": **factual error** (any specific number), **logical gap** ("therefore"/"so"),
   **false confidence** (smoothest lines), **missing context** (what an expert asks first),
   **fabricated source** (look up every citation), **stale fact** (prices/rules/versions).
   **Kills:** confident-sounding wrongness.
4. **Thinking in Systems** — *Cascade maps & loops.* Write the decision in one sentence; list
   five affected groups; ask "and then what?" three layers deep; find at least one **loop**
   where a later effect circles back and makes the decision worse. If it looks clean, you
   stopped too early. **Kills:** ignored side-effects.
5. **First Principles** — *Find the boundary.* When everyone (incl. AI) agrees, that's the
   danger — the common answer is the average of what worked for *most*; yours may be the
   exception. Name the **threshold** where the advice breaks for you ("when >80% of members
   have no income, a fee halves membership"), not a shrug ("fees aren't always good").
   **Kills:** the average answer instead of the right-for-you one.
6. **Working WITH AI** — *Override & iterate.* Do the task three ways — **Solo / AI-only /
   Together** — and lay them side by side; Together wins, and you can point at exactly where
   your judgment improved it. Keep your judgment in charge. **Kills:** your judgment quietly
   fading until you can't tell which parts are yours.

## Scale to the decision (don't use all six on lunch)
- Trivial → none, just decide.
- Somewhat → Prediction Lock + an error scan (10-15 min).
- Important w/ deadline → + Reasoning Receipt + Error Taxonomy + one or two more (30-60 min).
- Very important (people will judge your reasoning) → all six (90+ min).

**The carry-out question:** *Does this make AI a more powerful tool in your hands — or make
you a slower version of the tool?* The disciplines aren't the deliverable; the evidence they
produce is.
